# Social-action book checks: V20 and V20 Dark Ages (light helper, 2026-11-11)

Mechanics only, in our own words. Refs are pdf / printed page. V20 social systems are under "Social Feats", V20 pdf 273-274 / pr 265-266. V20DA repeats them under "Dramatic Systems", pdf 339-340 / pr 338-339.

## 1. Seduction (V20 pdf 274 / pr 266; V20DA pdf 340 / pr 339)
Three stages in a row. Each stage is a single roll, not extended.
- **Approach:** Appearance + Subterfuge, difficulty = target's Wits + 3. Each success past the first adds a die to stage 2. A failure means the target shows no interest; a botch means disgust or anger.
- **Witty repartee:** Wits + Subterfuge, difficulty = target's Intelligence + 3. Each success past the first adds a die to stage 3. A failure ends the contact for now, but the target may be open to it later. In V20DA a botch also gives the target a dangerous insight about the character.
- **Intimate talk:** Charisma + Empathy, difficulty = target's Perception + 3. Success means the target is smitten and goes somewhere private with the character. V20DA says a failure here is like stage 2's. A botch means a drink in the face.
- The target makes no resisting roll; the difficulties come from the target's traits.

## 2. Intimidation (V20 pdf 274 / pr 266; V20DA pdf 339 / pr 338, identical)
- **Passive:** no roll. A higher rating means people give the character more room.
- **Subtle** (an implied threat): Manipulation + Intimidation at difficulty 6, resisted by the target's Willpower at difficulty 6. The target needs more successes, or is cowed.
- **Overt** (a physical threat): Strength + Intimidation at difficulty 6, resisted by the higher of the target's Willpower or Strength + Intimidation.
- **Botch:** the character looks ridiculous and impresses no one for the rest of the scene.

## 3. Other social systems (V20 pdf 273-274 / pr 265-266; V20DA pdf 339 / pr 338 matches)
- **Carousing:** Charisma + Empathy, usually difficulty 6. It can be higher for a large or surly group, and Natures such as Bon Vivant or Curmudgeon can shift it. Not resisted. A botch means the character comes off as a boor, or people notice the character isn't eating or drinking.
- **Credibility** (a scam):
  - Manipulation + Subterfuge to run the scam, or Perception + Subterfuge to spot one, usually difficulty 7.
  - The mark needs more successes than the scammer to see through it.
  - Props and forged papers make the scam harder to see through; teamwork helps reveal it.
  - A botch collapses the scheme. In V20DA, a botch on detection means the mark falls for it badly.
- **Fast-Talk:** Manipulation + Subterfuge, resisted by the target's Willpower, both at difficulty 6. Most successes wins; on a tie the character keeps talking. A botch angers the target.
- **Interrogation, peaceful:** Manipulation + Empathy, resisted by Willpower, both at difficulty 6. Rolled at key points, every few minutes or at the end of a session.
- **Interrogation, violent:** Manipulation + Intimidation, resisted by the higher of Stamina + 3 or Willpower. Rolled every minute or turn.
  - The target loses one health level per turn of physical torture, or one temporary Willpower per turn of mental torture.
  - Interrogators can pool successes, even mixing Empathy and Intimidation (good cop, bad cop).
  - Each net success yields one more piece of information. If net successes exceed the target's permanent Willpower, the target tells everything.
  - A botch can destroy the subject's body or mind.
- **Oration:** Charisma + Leadership, usually difficulty 6, raised for a huge, cynical or hostile audience. It simply succeeds or fails; a botch can hurt the speaker's reputation or provoke an attack.
  - A prepared speech allows Intelligence + Expression at difficulty 7. Success lowers the oration difficulty by 1; a botch raises it.
- **Performance:** Charisma + Performance, difficulty 7, adjusted for the audience's mood and the piece's complexity. One success is pleasant; more are memorable. A botch is a flubbed show.
- **Persuasion:** neither book has a separate system for it. The nearest are Oration, Fast-Talk and Credibility.

## 4. Pickpocketing (V20)
- There is no dedicated system. Pickpocketing is listed only as a specialty of Larceny (V20 pdf 113 / pr 105).
- The nearest written system is Intrusion, Dexterity or Perception + Larceny, which covers locks, safes and alarms (V20 pdf 268 / pr 260).
- No resisting roll is given. A sensible reading is Dexterity + Larceny against the target's Perception + Alertness, but that is our suggestion, not book text.

## 5. Bribery
- **V20:** no rule found. Contacts are described as people you can bribe or coerce for information (V20 pdf 120 / pr 112).
- **V20DA:** no rule found. Bribery is a specialty of a court or politics Ability (V20DA pdf 175 / pr 174), and Wits is described as spotting the best bribe (pdf 163 / pr 162).
- So a bribe would be a Storyteller call, for example Manipulation + that Ability, backed by Resources.

## 6. Haggling
- **V20:** no system. Etiquette lists haggling among its uses (V20 pdf 112 / pr 104).
- **V20DA:** Commerce covers buying, selling, judging goods and negotiating prices (V20DA pdf 167 / pr 166).
- The sample roll is Manipulation + Commerce at difficulty 6 to talk a price down (V20DA pdf 328 / pr 327). It is not written as a resisted roll.

## 7. V20 Dark Ages equivalents
- **Seduction and Intimidation:** the same pools and difficulties as V20 (see 1 and 2), with the small botch and failure additions noted above.
- **Pickpocketing:** Legerdemain covers sleight of hand, picking pockets and juggling (V20DA pdf 166 / pr 165). The Alertness write-up notes that sleight-of-hand attempts fall under Legerdemain (pdf 170 / pr 169).
  - The sample roll is Dexterity + Legerdemain at difficulty 8 to lift a key from a guard's belt (V20DA pdf 329 / pr 328).
  - No resisting roll is written.

## 8. Siren-style hunting (V20 pdf 267-268 / pr 259-260; V20DA pdf 335 / pr 334)
- Hunting is one roll per hour, using whatever pool fits the method. The book's own example for seducing prey at a nightclub is Appearance + Subterfuge. That is the Siren link, and it matches stage 1 of Seduction.
- **V20 difficulty by area:** 4 slum or the Rack, 5 low-income or bohemian, 6 downtown or a warehouse district, 7 suburb, 8 heavily patrolled.
- **V20DA difficulty by area:** 4 slums or the Circus, 5 lower-class, 6 market, 7 merchant-class, 8 heavily patrolled.
- **Modifiers:**
  - Fame and Domain lower the difficulty by 1 per dot, to a minimum of 3.
  - Herd adds 1 die per dot.
  - The difficulty is higher for inhuman vampires: Nosferatu, some Gangrel, Humanity 4 or below, or a Path.
- **Results:** success means prey is caught and the vampire drinks as much as wanted, or one die's worth. A failure wastes the hour; a botch causes a complication.
- "Siren" itself appears in V20 only as a slang term for a vampire who seduces and sips (V20 pdf 42 / pr 34) and as a bloodline name. It has no pool of its own.
