# wodx-chronicle changelog

Newest first. Moved from the module table in docs/MODULES.md on 12 November 2026; add each new release at the top.

- 0.77.0 (Taxes on the month's upkeep: the Law here card tells a player how big the usual taxes are where they live, and the owner can keep them back (the law may notice, and a caught dodger feeds the law's heat). Dated levies take more on top: the poll taxes of 1377-1381, the Hussite and Thirty Years' War contributions, the French crown's debts, the hut tax, war taxes, requisitions and collectives. A levy weighs on every town of the land, and a crushing one brings riots and risings. A seat in a tax office hides the collector's own dodging. Each line gets a hook for what taxes mean to it)
- 0.76.0 (Standing before the law: a character's social rank now counts before the law of the land. Until their privileges or bars end on each land's dates, the unfree face harder trials and sentences and may hold no office of the state above the lowest; the clergy may claim the church's lighter hand; nobles are tried by their peers. In a modern age the wealthy buy the best advocates. A world setting chooses as history (the default), softened (no bar on offices) or off. A player character's gender never counts)
- 0.75.0 (Papers and the undying: each land and age keeps its own records of everyone (rolls, parish registers, household registers, censuses, passports, fingerprints, identity cards, citizen's numbers, computer records, biometrics). A new self on paper is a downtime project whose difficulty and goal grow with the records; when it is done the new name goes on the character. On a clock jump, a Kindred, ghoul, mummy or revenant who has kept one self for ten years or more may be noticed, on the Storyteller's card only. The Law here card lists the records kept for anyone with two dots of Law, Politics or Streetwise)
- 0.74.0 (Sentences: after a guilty verdict the Storyteller picks a sentence from what the land's law allows for deeds that grave, or shows mercy. The sentence stays on the character as a mark: a fine, a record in a parish register, a police file or a database by the age, time in prison, the galleys, a camp or a penal colony, a brand, banishment or outlawry in that land, or a death sentence for the Storyteller to play out. The Law here card tells a player their own marks in force there)
- 0.73.0 (Trials: after an arrest the Storyteller gets a card with the forms of trial the land allows that year: ordeal, trial by combat (fought with WoDX Combat), compurgation, an inquiry that needs a confession, a judge, a jury, or a show trial. The roll goes through WoDX Storyteller's call for a roll, open to the table, with the accused's seat on a court and any bribe counted. The verdict is posted and passed on for the sentence)
- 0.72.0 (The law's heat: a crime that witnesses see raises the law's heat on the culprit in that land, by how grave the deed is under that land's law and how well its law catches people. Heat cools by the age's means (weeks with a watchman, months with a police file, a year with cameras and databases). The Storyteller gets a card at Asked after, Wanted and Manhunt, with what it means for that kind of character and buttons to make the arrest or let it go. A player with a dot of Law, Politics or Streetwise sees their own standing on the Law here card. A new world setting turns it off)
- 0.71.0 (My characters (B3534-B3536): a roster window for each player, from the Clock or a key: the main character and every ghoul, Kinfolk, retainer, apprentice, thrall, follower and ally they own, each awake, in torpor, dead or away. One click plays another and selects its token. When the played character falls into torpor, dies or goes away, the roster opens to pick someone else. A player asks to add a character; the Storyteller approves and picks the sheet. The Storyteller marks a character away for a long journey; Referee ticket (B3520-B3523): a player ticks "ask for a ruling" in Ask the Storyteller for an action the rules do not settle. The Storyteller's client builds one ticket in the approval queue from both sheets: the pool, the crossover referee's case for a named power, and the risks by line and era book (Paradox, a sin against Humanity or the Road, Banality, Rage, Angst, witnesses). The card has Change difficulty (1 to 3 either way) and Complication (a foreshadowing from the story aids). Approving whispers the player a Roll button at the ruled difficulty; a ticket never passes on its own. House rule "Pause the game while a ruling waits", off by default)
