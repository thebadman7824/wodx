# WoDX Otherworlds 0.2.0 (Phases 2 and 5)

Part of the World of Darkness Expanded project. Private use only; not for distribution or sale. Runs only in worlds whose `world.json` carries `flags.wodx`.

## What it does

- **Ratings on scenes and regions.** Each scene can be tagged with its kind of place (town, village, wilderness, caern and so on), plus an optional Gauntlet override, a modifier, Shroud, Banality, whether a reflective surface is at hand, and an Umbral storm. A region with the "WoDX: Otherworld ratings" behavior overrides its scene. The Storyteller tags scenes in **Otherworld ratings by scene** (ghost button in the token controls). That window can suggest tags from scene names, fill them from a preset (Rothenstein ships with one), let battlemaps follow their scene, and write a report journal.
- **The era's chart.** The Gauntlet comes from the chart of the era in force (WoDX Chronicle): Dark Ages: Werewolf before 1601, the Wyld West book in the Wild West, and W20 from the Victorian age on. The charts, with every WoDX choice marked, are written to the Chronicle Rules journal as "Gauntlet charts".
- **Step sideways and peek** (token controls, for the selected Garou). Rolls Gnosis by the era's rules: the reflective surface, the Dark Ages penalty for repeated tries, the W20 hour of waiting, shift time, the pack's opener of the way, Caught and Lost, and the Umbral storm's double 1s. Results go to chat with the dice. Statuses: In the Umbra, Caught in the Gauntlet, Lost in the Pattern Web.
- **Penumbra prototype** (in the ratings window). Copies the scene you are viewing and adds a Penumbra Scene Level, so lighting, sound and sight can be tested per layer. Stepping sideways there moves the token onto the Penumbra level and back.

## Phase 5: the twin worlds

- **Penumbra and Shadowlands levels** (clone button in the token controls, Storyteller). Each world map gets a Penumbra level (elevation 1000) and a Shadowlands level (2000) showing its twin night map from `Data/wodx-assets/twins` (listed in `manifest.json` there). Roofs, props and lights are copied onto each twin level: roofs with their twin art, lights colder and a little dimmer. Travel regions are copied too, so a road out of one map's Penumbra leads to the next map's Penumbra; a twin road stays switched off until the far side has its twin. Walls, sounds and other regions stay on every level. Each scene is backed up to `Data/wodx-backups/scenes` before it changes; building again only adds what is missing. Removing twins asks first and puts everything back on every level.
- **Stepping sideways** moves the token to the Penumbra level and back, keeping its height above the floor.
- **Cross the Shroud** (skull button, Storyteller). Moves the selected tokens to the Shadowlands level and back, with the place's Shroud rating and how they crossed on a whispered card. No roll is made; each power has its own (Phase 6). A Garou cannot step sideways from the Shadowlands.
- **Disconnection** (W20 p. 319). Each crossing is recorded on the actor. Each lunar cycle spent in one world adds +1 to crossing to the other. After a year and a day in the Umbra, the Storyteller gets a proposal in the approval queue.
- **Umbral paths.** In the twin levels window: a Teleport Token region on two scenes' Penumbra levels only, so only travelers in the Umbra take it.
- **Read the Soul** (sparkling hand button). Select the reader, target the one read. Auspex 2; Perception + Empathy at difficulty 8 (V20 Dark Ages p. 195). The roll goes to the Storyteller only; the player sees what the soul shows, by successes: shade, main colour, patterns, subtle shifts, mixtures. A botch shows a false reading. The aura is drawn around the token for the reader alone, for 20 seconds. The Storyteller sets each character's colours with the palette button; vampire, ghoul, werebeast, witch, ghost and fae are read from the sheet, and frenzy from its condition.
- **One Umbra** (rule B31). Where the lines' ideas of the spirit world conflict, Wraith comes first, then Werewolf, then Mage. The conflicts decided so far are listed in Chronicle Rules, "Gauntlet charts".

## 0.12.0: a layout of its own for every realm

Every realm map now has its own layout, built from what the realm is in the books rather than which family of shapes it falls in: Phoebe's islands strung on an arc round her moon with the Moon Paths running to its rim; Arcadia's gateway a deep wood with round glades, a ring of standing stones and the silver road winding through; the Atrocity Realm's fenced yards and gibbet grove; the Dream Zone stitched from unrelated dreams; Erebus' terraces down to the Crucible; the Scar's rail lines fanning from the Last Junction; the Horizon realm's mirrored formal estate; the Paradox Realm's nested walls with one true door each; Autochthonia's sealed circuit; the CyberRealm's street lattice of towers and skybridges; Stygia packed with city to its sea wall, avenues from the Onyx Tower to the piers; Yomi's courts of the Yama Kings round Ti Yu; Duat's river journey past the gates to the hall of judgement; and more. No two realms share a layout (the Battleground's trenches, Malfeas' spiral and the Digital Web's grid stay with the one realm each fits), and every place on a map can be reached from the arrival. Keywords, the year and the realm's features still vary each map on top. Layouts: `REALM_LAYOUTS` in scripts/realm-layouts.mjs; each realm names its own with `layout`.

## 0.11.0: map keywords

Realm maps are now drawn with keywords, each one a kind of feature with its own step on the ground and its own marks in the art: the land (forest, marsh, peaks, caves, sea, ash and sand, ice, chasms, stars), what was built (ruins, towers, machines, webbing, tombs, gardens and fields) and what burns, rots or moves (fire, rot, storm, shifting, holes into nothing, bones, crystal). Every realm has two to four, so realms with the same features look alike: Stygia and the other Dark Kingdoms share towers and tombs, Pangaea and the Summer Country share woods and marsh. The seed sets how much of each and where, so no two maps are the same. Some keywords change the layout itself: islands turn angular with crystal, square with towers or gardens, scattered with shifting, cracked with chasms, ringed with rock with caves; a maze realm with caves becomes grown caverns; a city with gardens or crystal is laid out in square streets (the Yellow Springs). The age adds its own (a Great Maelstrom's storm, the Endless Winter's ice), some eras add more (the Battleground's machines in the future, the Legendary Realm's rot), and the Storyteller can add any in the Realm maps window ("forest:2, ruins"; 2 or 3 for more). The scene's note lists the keywords a map was drawn with. Rules: `KEYWORDS`, `parseKeys`, `realmKeys` in scripts/realm-maps-rules.mjs.

## 0.10.0: realm maps that follow their descriptions

Each realm map was checked against what the books say the realm looks like, and redrawn where it did not fit. Every landmark is placed once, and the arrival and the two ways out carry their own names. Stygia and the Dark Kingdoms are walled cities on islands in the Sea of Shadows, with the harbour as the arrival and the Onyx Tower or the throne at the heart. The Tempest and the Far Shores are islands in a storm sea, not on nothing. The Battleground starts at the Signpost, whose two paths part at once; its works follow the year (camps and charges before 1500, redoubts and cannon to 1913, trenches and shell craters after). Erebus and Duat have a river crossing the map, molten silver in Erebus. A black train line runs from Malfeas and the Scar to the map's edge. Enoch has its five spires in an arc and a gate down to the Labyrinth, Yomi has Ti Yu at its heart, the Aetherial Realm has Phoebe in the sky and the Wyrm Reaches along its top, Wolfhome has its Town, Horizon and Shard Realms have a walled Chantry, Duat has Amenti's hidden fortress, and the Labyrinth is strewn with broken machinery. Hazards are spread out, each one once. In the Shadowlands reflection, only the most recent house that stood on each spot comes back as a relic, so rebuilt streets are not stacked three deep.

## 0.9.0: Umbral twins from the town generator

A map made by the WoDX town generator carries a file beside it (name.wodx.json) that says what each place means. The **Reflect** button (token controls, Storyteller) reads it and draws that meaning on the scene's Penumbra and Shadowlands levels, making plain tinted ones if the scene has none. In the Penumbra churches and the market cross loom, rich wards and later the railway and offices carry the Weaver's webbing (thicker in each age), dirty trades, burned plots, bomb sites and neglected graveyards sour toward the Wyrm, and water and parks grow wild. In the Shadowlands, buildings later ages pulled down, and burned houses, stand again as relics, churches and cemeteries thin the Shroud, and bomb sites may open a Nihil. Each layer gets one overlay tile and regions for the marks that matter in play; running it again replaces them, and "Take the reflection off" removes them. Overlays are SVGs in Data/wodx-assets/twins/reflect. Rules: scripts/reflect-rules.mjs. API: `api.reflect` (`scene`, `undo`, `dialog`, `guessPath`, `rules`).

## 0.8.0: realm maps

Every realm past the Penumbra, the Shadowlands and the Near Dreaming can have a map of its own: 34 realms over eight lines, from Pangaea and Malfeas to Stygia, the Labyrinth, Yomi, Duat, the Digital Web and the Deep Dreaming. The **Realm maps** button (token controls, Storyteller) draws one from a seed for the year in force as a scene in the folder WoDX realms, with its walls (the edge of a void or the storm stops feet, not eyes) and regions for the arrival, the ways out, the landmarks and the hazards. The scene's note gives the realm's state that year, its ways in and its sources. Select tokens and send them in; they land on the arrival and remember where they stood, and "Bring them back" returns them. The art is an SVG in Data/wodx-assets/realms. Rules: scripts/realm-maps-rules.mjs. API: `api.realmMaps` (`draw`, `redraw`, `send`, `bringBack`, `dialog`, `isRealmMap`, `modelOf`, `rules`).

## Standing with the spirit courts (0.14.0)

Each character who deals with spirits keeps a standing from -5 to +5 with the Bird, Beast, Elemental, Wyld, Weaver and Wyrm courts and their totem (a house rule, B1320 to B1324). The Storyteller records what moves it in the Spirits window (the feather button with the token tools): chiminage paid or refused, a Bane destroyed, a spirit forced, a Ban kept or broken. Standing changes the difficulty to summon, learn a Gift from, or bind a spirit of that court in WoDX Actions; at -5 the court may refuse. Players see their own standing in words. API: `api.spirits.of(actor)`, `standing(actor, court)`, `effect(actor, court)`, `move(actor, key, {court, by, why})`, `dialog(actor)`.
