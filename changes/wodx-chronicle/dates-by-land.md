Dates as each land tells them (B5870-B5876): the era reckoning house rule now follows the year kept where the scene stands. Orthodox lands count from the Creation of the World until 1700 (Greece 1821), then print the Julian date as Old Style until their reform; the lands of Islam tell the day and month of the Hijra; the Jewish year counts from the Creation; China, Korea and Japan tell the lunar day and month with the year by stem and branch and its animal, China by the years of the Republic from 1912 and Japan by the emperor's era from 1868; Tibet by element and animal; India by the bright and dark halves of the lunar month in the Vikrama year (the Saka year in the islands); the Buddhist south by the waxing and waning moon of the numbered month in the year of the Buddha; peoples with a count of their own by the night of the moon and the season. Each land's civil date leads from the year it was taken up, with the old count in brackets. Before clocks the hour is told by the five prayers, the twelve double hours, the watches of the day and night, or the sun's course; striking clocks reach the lands outside Christendom in 1700.
